## Application question

What experience and meaning do you hope players take away from your game, and how is that reflected in its design?

## Submitted answer

I want the player to feel the immense freedom of having a deck under their feet and a whole huge world to explore. I want them to feel the thrill of exploration. I want a trip from Lisbon to the Spice Islands to feel like an achievement and a meaningful journey. I want the ocean to feel huge and enticing, but also risky: a bad wind could leave you in the middle of the ocean without enough water to survive. A pirate could engage and sink you off the coast of Mozambique. Death is permanent and losing your character matters.

The main feature that contributes to the sense of exploration and freedom is the absolutely massive Earth map. It has detailed coastlines, biomes, rivers, and historical city and village locations. The player can take Magellan's route around the world and land in the same places his crew did. Each region has different quests, different architecture, different trade goods, different ships, different wild animals to discover. So going around the world really matters: there is new and different content around the next coastline.

Finally, once players have explored the world and built up their ship and their wealth, I want them to feel like they can bend and change this world to their will, to engage in the fantasy of making history play out differently. They can do this through colonization and conquest, changing the timing of historical events or even rewriting them completely.

I want players to come away from the game feeling like they have a deeper understanding of world history in the 1520s. The game has a lot of content drawn directly from the history of the period. From Ming dynasty export restrictions to Martin Luther printing the New Testament in German (which the player may smuggle, in contravention of the Edict of Worms) to the internal politics of the Sengoku Jidai, to the Spice Island rivalry between Ternate and Tidore, there's a ton of real history for the player to discover through gameplay.
