## Submitted answer

Marque & Reprisal is a massive roguelike sandbox that threads the needle between deep historical simulation and simple, accessible gameplay.

The game is designed with a specific player profile in mind: It's someone who loves history, but doesn't enjoy the extreme UI complexity that characterizes historical grand strategy games.

The core game loop is about choosing a heading for your sailing ship, sailing until you reach a port, tacking to move upwind if necessary, and then interacting with menus to accept quests or trade goods. A non-gamer can learn it in five minutes.

But this simple sailing game happens within a massive world simulation that the player can meaningfully interact with. The Earth is a massive Goldberg polyhedron with 655,362 tiles and over 300 cities. The prices of goods react to supply and demand and specie flows. There are 73 factions with distinct foreign policies, which the player can impact by ferrying emissaries around, or by directly taking part in wars. The player can personally create new colonial cities in the New World by helping to provision the colonists and then transporting them across the Atlantic. The player can invest in shipyards, provision and manage them, and turn huge profits. The player can go from a small fishing boat to earning millions of doubloons and becoming a creditor to kings and emperors.

Player characters are random and procedural. A player can be born as a Spanish Roman Catholic man in Seville or a Shinto-Buddhist Japanese woman from Kyoto or an Eastern Orthodox Greek woman under Ottoman rule in Athens or a Sunni Muslim man from Ternate.

And the characters also have different goals. Some want to pay off a family debt, others want to discover the wonders of the world, others want to get revenge against a white whale, others want to find pirate treasure. The player character's identity, goal, and general RNG combine to make every run unique.

This is a huge game with a ton of emergent gameplay that is already engrossing playtesters.
