# Trade Specialties, 1522

The economy uses named production centers to make nearby ports feel distinct. Regional
profiles now provide mostly staples; valuable manufactures and characteristic exports
begin scarce outside their source ports and spread through NPC trade.

These goods are deliberately less lucrative than intercontinental spices:

| Good | Principal sources represented in game |
| --- | --- |
| Amber | Gdansk |
| Furs and beeswax | Novgorod, Pskov, Kholmogory |
| Naval stores | Riga, Szczecin, Stockholm |
| Paper and printed books | Chinese paper centers and European printing cities |
| Ginseng | Kaesong and Seoul |
| Lacquerware | Kyoto, Sakai, and Gyeongju |
| Sulfur | Kagoshima, Hakata, and Yamaguchi |

The Baltic loop sends grain, timber, amber, furs, wax, metals, and naval stores toward
western commercial ports, while cloth, salt, wine, arms, and books move east. The East
Asian loop distinguishes Jingdezhen porcelain, Jiangnan silk, Korean paper and ginseng,
and Japanese lacquerware, silver, and sulfur.

Historical anchors:

- The Hanseatic League describes Novgorod exports including furs, wax, honey, and wood,
  exchanged for cloth, salt, wine, silver, and other western goods:
  <https://www.hanse.org/en/the-medieval-hanseatic-league/die-kontore>
- UNESCO identifies Jingdezhen as the Ming porcelain center whose wares entered domestic
  and international trade:
  <https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6265/>
- Sweden's History Museum describes medieval iron and copper as foundations of the
  Swedish export economy:
  <https://historiska.se/en/explore-history/history-hub/iron-and-copper-mining-in-the-middle-ages/>
- Korea's official tourism history notes Korean ginseng exports to neighboring countries
  from early periods through Joseon:
  <https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=221334>
- Japan's National Diet Library catalogs research on the large East Asian circulation of
  Japanese sulfur and silver in the Muromachi and Sengoku periods:
  <https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-I031300139>
- The Metropolitan Museum of Art surveys the established Chinese, Korean, and Japanese
  lacquer traditions:
  <https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/lacquerware-of-east-asia>
