# Trade restrictions represented in 1522

The voyage opens on 21 March 1522. The simulation distinguishes three acts that older code
had conflated:

- an **enemy-import ban** follows merchandise from its sovereign place of origin;
- a **strategic-export prohibition** is broken when controlled goods are furnished to the
  prohibited destination;
- a **naval blockade** follows the allegiance or destination of shipping.

This is the game-relevant catalog for represented powers and navigable trade. Portuguese
cartazes, Iberian colonial monopolies, Ming port restrictions, and local customs rules remain
in their own systems because they are not interstate embargoes.

## In force at the opening

- France bars English merchandise. Francis I had already detained English shipping and
  property before Henry VIII answered with his declaration of war.
- Spain, the Burgundian Netherlands, and the Habsburg hereditary lands bar French merchandise
  during the continuing Habsburg-Valois war.
- The Holy See's standing prohibition bars Christians from furnishing naval stores, metals,
  arms, powder, matchlocks, and sailcloth to the Ottoman state. This is an export rule, not a
  ban on buying Ottoman carpets or spices. Catholic rulers decide whether to follow it, and the
  Pope and those rulers may later relax it through the political simulation.

## Dated changes during 1522

- **29 May:** England declares war upon France and bars French merchandise.
- **1 June:** Suleiman's warning of the expedition against Rhodes begins the open
  Ottoman-Hospitaller war; the Hospitallers begin enforcing a blockade of Ottoman shipping.
- **June (represented on 1 June):** Lubeck enters the Danish war and closes the Sound to
  Danish shipping. This is represented as a blockade rather than a fictional Hanseatic
  national customs policy; the source establishes the 1522 campaign, not a day-specific decree.

Each dated change applies only if the relevant states and relationship still survive in their
opening condition. A conquest, peace, alliance, or active negotiation marks the historical
change as averted instead of overwriting the voyage's divergent history. Migrated saves retain
the exact wars and embargo orders already established in that voyage.

## Deliberate omissions

- Selim I's embargo on Safavid silk is absent: Suleiman released the Persian merchants and
  restored commerce on his accession in 1520.
- The Hospitallers do not begin the March voyage with a blanket ban on all Ottoman wares. Their
  June blockade is tied to the Rhodes campaign.
- Papal policy does not make ordinary Ottoman-origin merchandise contraband. It controls
  strategic exports to the prohibited power.
