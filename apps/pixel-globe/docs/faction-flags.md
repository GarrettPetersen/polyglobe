# Faction Flags and Heraldry, 1522

These 32x20 pixel identifiers distinguish documented contemporary banners from heraldic substitutes and explicit reconstructions. They are game assets, not claims that every polity used a modern national flag.

All pixels use the Resurrect 64 palette. Regenerate with `npm run render:faction-flags` from `apps/pixel-globe/`.

| Faction | Representation | Evidence | Accuracy note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neutral | Neutral UI marker | `ui-symbol` | A deliberately non-historical gray marker for unaffiliated ports and ships. |
| Pirates | Black rogue pennant | `ui-symbol` | A generic black early-modern rogue pennant. It avoids the later standardized Jolly Roger iconography. |
| Kingdom of England | Royal banner of England, 1406-1603 | `period-banner` | The Tudor royal banner quarters the three lions of England with the three fleurs-de-lis of France. Sources: [Royal standard of England (1406-1603)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Royal_standard_of_England_(1406%E2%80%931603).svg). |
| Kingdom of Scotland | Royal Banner of Scotland | `period-banner` | The red lion rampant within the double tressure on gold was the royal banner throughout the period. Sources: [Royal Banner of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Banner_of_Scotland). |
| Kingdom of France | France moderne royal banner | `period-banner` | Blue with three gold fleurs-de-lis, the royal arms in use after Charles V. Sources: [History of the French flag](https://www.drapeaux-sfv.org/drapeaux-de-france/article/l-histoire-du-drapeau-francais). |
| Spanish Monarchy | Cross of Burgundy | `period-banner` | The ragged red Cross of Burgundy was introduced to Habsburg Spain in 1506 and is the clearest military and naval identifier for 1522. Sources: [Cross of Burgundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_of_Burgundy). |
| Kingdom of Portugal | Royal arms of John III | `period-heraldry` | A white royal banner carrying the Portuguese shield. The tiny raster simplifies the quinas and castle border. Sources: [Historical flags of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Portugal#History). |
| Habsburg Monarchy | Imperial banner of Charles V | `period-banner` | The black double-headed imperial eagle on gold, with a small Habsburg breast shield. Sources: [The Eagle of the Holy Roman Empire](https://www.flagheritagefoundation.org/web/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/DoubleEagle-Final-PDF-150dpi.pdf). |
| Kingdom of Hungary | Arpad stripes | `period-heraldry` | The red and silver Arpad stripes were long-established Hungarian royal arms and are less speculative than a modern national flag. Sources: [Historical flags of Europe](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Historical_flags_of_Europe). |
| Ottoman Empire | Three-crescent Ottoman naval standard | `near-period-banner` | A red field with three crescents, based on surviving descriptions and near-period Ottoman naval imagery. Ottoman standards varied; this is not presented as a single national flag. Sources: [Battle of Lepanto, 1571](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Battle_of_Lepanto_1571.jpg). |
| Republic of Venice | Lion of Saint Mark | `period-heraldry` | The winged lion of Saint Mark on red was the enduring Venetian state and naval emblem. Sources: [Flag of the Republic of Venice](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Flag_of_the_Republic_of_Venice.svg). |
| Republic of Genoa | Cross of Saint George | `period-banner` | The red Saint George cross on white is directly visible among Genoese naval standards in early-modern battle imagery. Sources: [Battle of Lepanto, 1571](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Battle_of_Lepanto_1571.jpg). |
| Papal States | Crossed Keys of Saint Peter | `near-period-heraldry` | The crossed gold and silver keys are period papal heraldry; the dark red field is a game-facing banner reconstruction rather than the later yellow-white state flag. Sources: [Papal States in the 16th century](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Papal_States_in_the_16th_century). |
| Ming Empire | Ming imperial dragon identifier | `period-ceremonial-reconstruction` | Ming records separately enumerate dragon flags and yellow ceremonial standards. Combining them here creates a legible imperial identifier, not a documented exact flag or a national flag. Sources: [History of Ming, Ceremonial Guards](https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?chapter=429783&if=en&remap=gb). |
| Aztec Empire | Eagle and cactus of Tenochtitlan | `period-emblem` | The Nahua-made Codex Mendoza preserves the eagle-on-cactus city emblem. No European-style Aztec national flag is implied. Sources: [Codex Mendoza frontispiece](https://smarthistory.org/frontispiece-of-the-codex-mendoza/). |
| Inca Empire | Inca royal standard | `documented-royal-standard` | A rigid square royal standard with the rainbow and paired serpents, following colonial-era descriptions of Inca regalia rather than the modern rainbow flag. Sources: [The Rainbow Flag of the Incas](https://www.fiav.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/ICV23-15-Tracchia-TheRainbowFlagOfTheIncas.pdf). |
| Safavid Empire | Lion and rising sun | `period-regional-emblem` | The lion-and-sun banner device is documented in Persian art by 1423. Its use here is a contemporary Persian dynastic equivalent, not the later 1576 Safavid flag. Sources: [1423 lion-and-sun banner](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Earliest_example_of_a_banner_bearing_the_lion_and_sun.png). |
| Grand Duchy of Muscovy | Seal of Ivan III | `period-emblem` | The double-headed eagle entered Muscovite state seals under Ivan III; the banner field is a reconstruction around that contemporary emblem. Sources: [Russian treaty seals](https://www.athensjournals.gr/history/2024-5981-AJHIS-ART-Ning-02.pdf). |
| Polish-Lithuanian Union | White Eagle and Pahonia | `period-heraldry` | A paired heraldic banner for the Jagiellonian union, combining the Polish White Eagle and Lithuanian mounted Pursuer. Sources: [Golden Age of the Jagiellonians](https://zlotaepoka.ossolineum.pl/en/). |
| Denmark-Norway | Dannebrog | `period-banner` | The white Scandinavian cross on red was already the Danish royal banner in the early sixteenth century. Sources: [Dannebrog history](https://lex.dk/Dannebrog). |
| Songhai Empire | Askia cavalry standard reconstruction | `reconstruction` | No reliable Songhai state flag or dynastic arms were found. This geometric Sahelian standard is intentionally marked as a reconstruction, using spear and river motifs rather than a fabricated modern flag. Sources: [Songhai Empire overview](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-2/pages/3-2-the-songhai-empire). |
| Wattasid Morocco | Alam al-mansur with Marinid octagram | `period-banner-tradition` | The Wattasids inherited Moroccan banner traditions: a white victorious standard and red unit banners. The gold octagram on red is the closest recognizable Marinid-Wattasid heraldic equivalent. Sources: [Alam al-mansur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%27alam_al-mans%C3%BBr), [Morocco historical flags](https://www.crwflags.com/fotw/flags/ma_hist.html). |
| Ethiopian Empire | Solomonic cross and lion | `reconstruction` | No rectangular Ethiopian national flag or exact graphic emblem is evidenced for 1522. This reconstruction uses Solomonic Christian kingship symbols while avoiding the nineteenth-century tricolor and imperial flag. Sources: [Lion of Judah royal insignia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_of_Judah). |
| Vijayanagara Empire | Varaha, sun, moon, and dagger | `period-emblem` | The royal insignia survives on Vijayanagara architecture and coins. The saffron field is a restrained banner reconstruction around those attested devices. Sources: [Vijayanagar crest emblem](https://govtmuseumchennai.org/museum-section/hindu-sculpture/vijayanagar-crest-emblem). |
| Gujarat Sultanate | Muzaffar Shah II coin device | `period-numismatic-emblem` | No secure Gujarat state flag was found. The roundel and angular calligraphy are adapted from coins struck by Muzaffar Shah II in the exact period. Sources: [Muzaffar Shah II coin](https://www.khalilicollections.org/islamic-coin-print/?post_id=26992). |
| Bengal Sultanate | Red lion on white | `near-period-banner` | The red lion banner appears in the Catalan Atlas and the lion remained a Bengali sultanic emblem; a 1521 account also describes guards carrying lion-painted shields. Sources: [Bengal Sultanate flag evidence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bengal_Sultanate_flag.svg). |
| Delhi Sultanate | Lodi black standard with coin roundel | `reconstruction` | No secure Lodi banner was found. Black references the Abbasid standard tradition reported for Delhi, while the gold roundel is adapted from sultanic coin calligraphy. Sources: [Delhi Sultanate flag traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate). |
| Ayutthaya Kingdom | Plain red Siamese ensign | `later-traditional-ensign` | Ayutthaya had no documented national flag in 1522. Plain red is the earliest recorded Siamese ensign, but the evidence begins under Narai in the later seventeenth century. Sources: [Thai Fine Arts Department flag history](https://www.finearts.go.th/storage/contents/2024/01/file/01sb4xTcSwsVGZ2fRvQiH76sPzDyOyZmmCdFB2yG.pdf). |
| Ashikaga Japan | Ashikaga futatsuhikiryo mon | `period-clan-heraldry` | The two-bar roundel is the Ashikaga clan crest, used here as shogunal heraldry rather than a Japanese national flag. Sources: [Ashikaga clan futatsuhikiryo](https://www.paxhistoria.co/flags/6a77b848-9ef3-444f-b1be-7f3166b633e4). |
| Joseon | Yellow Dragon Flag | `period-royal-standard` | Joseon used a four-clawed dragon flag in royal processions. It represents the king's authority, not a modern Korean national flag. Sources: [Korean dragon flag tradition](https://smarthistory.org/korean-national-flag/), [Joseon Legendary Dragon Flag](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/legendary-dragon-flag/pAG5Om1riEle4g?hl=en). |
