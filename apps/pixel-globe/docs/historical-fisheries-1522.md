# Historical fisheries in 1522

The fish simulation distinguishes ordinary waters from historically prominent
commercial fishing grounds. These values are gameplay abstractions rather than
literal biomass estimates.

## Historical basis

- The sixteenth-century North Atlantic "fish revolution" brought a major rise in
  fish supplies. Newfoundland was the true step change, while Norway, Iceland,
  the Faroes, the North Sea, Ireland, and Iberia already supported mature
  commercial fisheries:
  <https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S0033589418001539/type/journal_article>
- Reconstructions of North Atlantic catches identify major cod and herring
  fisheries from about 1520 in the North Sea, around Ireland, western Norway,
  the Faroes, Iceland, and Newfoundland:
  <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/faf.12598>
- Archaeological and historical evidence indicates that intensive late-medieval
  European fishing had already altered the abundance, size, and distribution of
  some stocks:
  <https://doi.org/10.1007/s10152-004-0203-5>
- Galicia exported substantial quantities of fish, especially sardines, during
  the sixteenth century:
  <https://portalcientifico.uvigo.gal/documentos/5d1df67129995204f766c2c1>
- Early European reports described the cod off Newfoundland as exceptionally
  abundant:
  <https://journal.nafo.int/Portals/0/1998/lear.pdf>

## Gameplay translation

- Ordinary marine fisheries appear less often and draw fewer fish.
- Long-exploited Old World waters begin with lower average density and capacity.
- Named productive grounds retain regional bonuses, with Newfoundland and the
  Grand Banks far above the rest.
- Mature Old World grounds remain useful but are less dramatic than Newfoundland.
- Existing depletion and recovery mechanics continue to operate locally, so
  repeated catches can exhaust even a productive tile.
