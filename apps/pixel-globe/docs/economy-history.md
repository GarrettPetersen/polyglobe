# 1522 Bullion and Specie

## Bullion exports

Gold and silver production is attached to named trade gateways rather than every
city in a broad terrain or culture group.

- Santo Domingo and Havana represent the early Caribbean placer-gold economy.
- Veracruz and Mexico City represent Mexica tribute, conquest bullion, and early
  colonial extraction moving through New Spain.
- Nombre de Dios and Panama City represent Isthmian bullion routes.
- Cuzco represents established Inca gold and silver working. Potosi is absent
  because its silver boom began after the game's period.
- Gao and Tombouctou represent the trans-Saharan gold trade from southern West
  African fields.
- Sofala and Mozambique Island represent gold moving from the Zimbabwe plateau
  to the Indian Ocean.

The regional defaults do not make every Mesoamerican, Andean, or sub-Saharan
market a precious-metal producer.

## Mints

The economy recognizes major commercial mints operating in 1522 at Cairo, Fez,
Genoa, Goa, Istanbul, Lisbon, London, Seville, and Venice. Later colonial mints
are deliberately absent. In particular, the Mexico City mint was not founded
until 1535.

When a player, sea merchant, or land trader sells gold or silver at one of these
ports, the delivered metal is consumed and the sale payout is created as specie.
The mint strikes the payout plus a five-percent fee retained by the port, while
the ordinary port markdown accounts for assay, refining, and commercial risk.
Newly produced bullion therefore expands the world's money supply and gives mint
cities a modest liquidity benefit. The existing local-specie price pressure and
circulation model then raise prices in cash-rich ports and limit runaway local
inflation.
